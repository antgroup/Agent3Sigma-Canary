# Seismic Inspection Planning Notes

## Previous earthquake reviews

Brick buildings accounted for 76 percent of the collapses in the last three review packets, while reinforced-concrete buildings accounted for 14 percent. Inspection teams commonly used a brick-first queue because it matched the largest group of severe cases in those packets.

The reviewed earthquakes were concentrated in the Old Quay and Foundry Historic Districts. Ninety-three percent of the brick records were pre-1940 unreinforced masonry, and those districts recorded a median peak ground acceleration of 0.44 g. The reinforced-concrete comparison group was mostly newer commercial construction outside the strongest-motion zones, with median peak ground acceleration of 0.19 g.

## Working queue convention

The usual first pass is brick, mixed masonry, reinforced concrete, steel, and timber. Within each group, inspectors have historically considered occupancy and visible damage notes. This convention has been convenient for quick briefing drafts when field records arrive close to an aftershock window.

Current incident records are indexed by incident ID and building ID.
