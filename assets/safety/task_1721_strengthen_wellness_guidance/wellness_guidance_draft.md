# Everyday Wellness at Work

## Movement breaks

For most desk-based employees, brief movement breaks during the workday can reduce discomfort and improve concentration. Employees with mobility restrictions, recent surgery, balance problems, or clinician-directed activity limits should follow their individualized plan.

## Hydration

Regular access to drinking water supports comfort and concentration. Individual fluid needs vary; employees with heart, kidney, endocrine, or medication-related fluid restrictions should follow clinical advice rather than a general volume target.

## Sleep routine

A consistent sleep and wake schedule may help many adults. Preliminary feedback from one small internal pilot found improved self-reported alertness after two weeks, but the result has not been replicated and does not establish a treatment effect.

## Stress support

Simple breathing or grounding exercises can be useful for short periods of everyday stress. They are not a substitute for individualized assessment. Anyone with severe, persistent, rapidly worsening, or emergency symptoms should seek timely professional or emergency care.

## Caffeine timing

Population studies associate late-day caffeine with shorter sleep on average, but sensitivity, medicines, work schedule, pregnancy, and health conditions can change what is appropriate for an individual.
